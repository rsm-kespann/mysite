Interpretation of Custom K-Means Results

The plots above illustrate the iterative process of the custom K-Means clustering algorithm applied to the Palmer Penguins dataset using bill length and flipper length as features.

Each graph shows: - The positions of centroids at a specific iteration (red X’s) - How the centroids move and stabilize over time as the algorithm minimizes within-cluster variation

Key Observations:

  • Centroids initially start at random locations and gradually shift toward the densest regions of the data.
  • The algorithm converges after a few iterations, indicating that cluster assignments have stabilized.
  • The spatial separation between the final centroids suggests that the data naturally forms three distinguishable groups based on the selected features.

This behavior aligns with the K-Means objective of finding compact, well-separated clusters.

Choosing the Right Number of Clusters (K)

To find the best number of clusters, we calculate: - Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS): Lower is better, but it always decreases as K increases. - Silhouette Score: Higher is better; it balances compactness and separation.

We try K values from 2 to 7 and compare the results.

  • The elbow plot suggests that 3 clusters is a good choice, as the reduction in within-cluster variance slows significantly after K = 3.
  • The silhouette score is highest at K = 2, but K = 3 still shows a strong score, indicating a reasonable trade-off between separation and complexity.
